Post-draft power rankings

Ranked by a composite of four measures, because any single one is easy to game: average ADP of the starting seven, median starter ADP (does one stud carry the lineup?), average ADP across the ten best skill players (depth), and positional value over replacement. A team has to be good on all four to finish high. Nobody has played a snap, and half of this will look foolish by October.

Wins the composite because it is the only roster without a soft spot. Justin Jefferson falling to 14 was luck; everything after it was not. The deepest roster in the field — best average ADP across ten skill players, which is the metric that actually survives a season. Christian McCaffrey at 10 is the highest-variance first-rounder in the draft and the only one whose owner bothered to buy the hedge. By the numbers

Six numbers that explain the draft

1.8

The first round was chalk

Twelve picks, an average miss of under two slots against ADP. The largest deviation was Saquon Barkley going seven picks early at the turn. By round twelve the average miss was 48.6 — the board stopped existing somewhere around the seventh.

4

Quarterbacks, one starting slot

michael prince drafted Josh Allen, Jaxson Dart, Patrick Mahomes and Baker Mayfield. Mahomes at 145 and Mayfield at 169 were both real value, which is the defense. The cost is three roster spots that cannot score points in a league that starts one QB.

8–4

Bingo Night went all in on receivers

Eight receivers to four running backs, the most lopsided build in the league, in a field where six teams drafted five or more backs. In full PPR that is a coherent strategy. It also means one running back injury away from starting a waiver add.

12

The turn was worth having

Thomas Fowler drew the twelfth pick, which in a snake means back-to-back selections in every round. He opened with Saquon Barkley and A.J. Brown on consecutive picks and closed by pairing a defense with a kicker. Eight pairs of decisions, no waiting.

20

Bowers set the tight end market

Brock Bowers came off the board at pick 20, seventeen slots early and first at the position. Trey McBride followed three picks later. After those two the position collapsed — Travis Kelce was still available at 132, and Mark Andrews at 128.

25

Allen went first, and early

Josh Allen at 25 was ten picks ahead of ADP and the first quarterback selected. Nine more went in the next 75 picks. The managers who waited — Mason on Prescott at 94, Lyudmil on Stafford at 155 — paid a fraction for comparable production.
